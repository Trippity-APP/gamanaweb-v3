# PART 1 — STRATEGIC DIRECTION

## 1.1 Project Goal

The goal is to **restructure the entire Gamana website without changing existing URLs**, so the website aligns with Gamana’s actual strategic direction, current product truth, and commercial growth priorities. The website should stop behaving like a broad AI travel product catalog and instead become a **confidence-first travel website** focused on helping the right audience understand why Gamana matters and download the app quickly. This direction is consistent with the strategy deck, which positions Gamana as a **travel companion, not a travel app**, centered on confidence, cultural clarity, trust, and offline readiness.

## 1.2 Core Business Priority

For the next 6 months, the website should primarily target:

**Outbound Indians**, especially Indians travelling to:

- Europe
- Southeast Asia
- Japan
- Turkey

This is fully aligned with the internal audience strategy, where outbound Indian travelers are a top-priority segment with needs around emotional safety, cultural decoding, clarity, embarrassment avoidance, and offline preparedness.

## 1.3 Secondary Business Priority

The website should also lean more strongly into **B2B partner acquisition**, especially for:

- OTAs
- package operators
- premium travel planners
- hotels
- destination/travel ecosystem partners

This is important because the internal strategy clearly frames Gamana’s B2B value around:

- reducing traveler friction
- reducing support calls
- improving satisfaction and reviews
- supporting itinerary and QR-driven usage
- creating premium partner differentiation.

## 1.4 Strategic Brand Shift

### Current website behavior

The live site behaves like:

- an AI-powered travel app
- a feature catalog
- a tours/offers marketplace
- a travel-tech product brochure

### Required website behavior

The website should instead behave like:

- a **confidence companion brand**
- a **trip-readiness and cultural clarity tool**
- a **download-first conversion system**
- a **partner acquisition platform**

## 1.5 Main Positioning Statement

The new website should revolve around this positioning:

**Gamana helps Indians travel abroad with confidence by giving them cultural clarity, useful context, and offline-ready audio guidance — while helping travel partners reduce friction and improve traveler experience.**

## 1.6 Core Messaging Pillars

Across the website, the messaging should consistently reinforce these pillars:

- Travel with confidence
- Understand what matters here
- Know what to notice
- Feel calm, prepared, and respectful
- Use it offline when needed
- Get useful cultural guidance without overwhelm
- Trust what you’re hearing

These pillars are directly supported by the strategy and gap-analysis documents.

# PART 2 — NON-NEGOTIABLE SITE RULES

## 2.1 URL Policy

All current URLs stay exactly as they are.

This includes:

- 11 core pages
- 8 feature pages
- 3 legal pages
- 26 blog posts

The sitemap should remain intact.

## 2.2 Blog Policy

The blog layer is protected.

### Rules:

- keep all blog slugs intact
- keep all blog article body copy completely untouched
- do not rewrite article content
- only improve surrounding template elements outside the article body

## 2.3 English Policy

The website should remain **English-first for now**.

Future geo-specific or language-specific variants can be planned later, but they should not complicate the current restructuring.

## 2.4 Claim Policy

The following claims can stay in principle:

- auto-trigger audio
- coins
- user-generated tours
- partner discounts
- supported languages

The following public brag metrics should **not** be used right now:

- number of partners
- number of active travelers

Because the business has launched recently, those numbers should not be pushed publicly yet.

## 2.5 Website Objective Hierarchy

Every important page should do one or more of the following:

1. Help the user understand whether Gamana is right for their trip
2. Make the user trust the product
3. Make app download easy
4. Help partners understand the B2B value
5. Move the visitor closer to app install or partner inquiry

If a page does not support one of those jobs, it should be rewritten, demoted, or simplified.

# PART 3 — WEBSITE ARCHITECTURE

## 3.1 Main Site Structure

The new website should revolve around **two clear funnels**.

### Funnel A — B2C

**Travel abroad with confidence**

Built primarily for:

- Indians travelling to Europe
- Indians travelling to Southeast Asia
- Indians travelling to Japan
- Indians travelling to Turkey
- later: USA and Australia travelers

### Funnel B — B2B

**Add a confidence layer to every trip**

Built for:

- OTAs
- package operators
- premium travel planners
- hotels
- travel ecosystem partners

## 3.2 Route-Based Discovery Framework

Instead of organizing the site only by city or feature, the website should use **trip routes/circuits** as major discovery and proof layers.

### Main route architecture

- Classic Europe Circuit
- Switzerland Alpine Circuit
- Italy Highlights Circuit
- Turkey Highlights Circuit
- Japan Golden Route
- Southeast Asia Confidence Route
- USA East Coast Circuit
- Australia East Coast Circuit

## 3.3 Southeast Asia Confidence Route

This is now a first-class strategic route and should be treated as such across the website.

### Included destinations:

- Vietnam
- Cambodia

### Positioning angle:

This route should be presented as a confidence-focused travel pathway for Indian travelers who want:

- cultural clarity
- useful context
- calm guidance
- faster understanding
- less confusion
- offline-ready support

Vietnam and Cambodia should not be treated like random extra countries inside a city index. They should be intentionally grouped as a **branded route**.
